<section class="mf-Section">
  <div class="mf-Inner">
    <h2 class="mf-Heading mf-Heading_Center">What we do today</h2>
    <div class="mf-Columns">
      <div>
        <h3 class="mf-Subheading">AWS for health tech</h3>
        <p>This is our established work. We build and run AWS infrastructure for startups and scale-ups with sensitive data, most of them health tech companies that handle patient data under HIPAA.</p>
        <p>Every project starts from our own blueprints: Right Start, a productized AWS landing zone, and an ECS blueprint for running containers in production. Security comes first in every design. We build the controls our customers' compliance work needs; the audit itself stays with their auditors.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3 class="mf-Subheading">AI forward-deployed engineering</h3>
        <p>This is our newer work. FivexL is a services partner in Anthropic's Claude Partner Network.</p>
        <p>Our engineers join customer teams to bring AI agents and AI features into production, on the same secure AWS foundations we build for everyone else.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mf-Section mf-Section_Grey">
  <div class="mf-Inner">
    <h2 class="mf-Heading mf-Heading_Center">What the name stands for</h2>
    <div class="mf-Columns mf-Columns_Values">
      <div>
        <h3 class="mf-Subheading">League</h3>
        <p>Being a solo consultant is lonely. We band together so we can support each other, achieve more, and open doors that stay closed to solopreneurs and freelancers. Nobody solves a hard problem alone: another senior engineer reviews the design, and what one of us learns, the rest of us can use.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3 class="mf-Subheading">Location-independent</h3>
        <p>We have no office and no commute. Our team spans many countries, and our customers are mostly in the US and Europe. We bring good work to talented people where they already live, so nobody has to move to a big city and trade their quality of life for a career. Everyone chooses where they work and how they plan their day, and shows up for their team and their customers.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3 class="mf-Subheading">Lovely</h3>
        <p>Life is too short to work with people you don't like, trust and respect. Experts are rare, so we are picky and hire slowly: we are building a company we ourselves want to work in. It isn't the right company for everyone, but it is a great one for the people in it.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3 class="mf-Subheading">Lifetime Learners</h3>
        <p>This one matters most. Everyone here loves to learn and get better, and a team like that can go wherever the work goes. Today it is AWS; tomorrow it may be something else, and we will learn it and master it. Along the way we turn what we learn into tools and blueprints we can reuse.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mf-Section">
  <div class="mf-Inner">
    <h2 class="mf-Heading mf-Heading_Center">How we work</h2>
    <div class="mf-Columns">
      <div>
        <h3 class="mf-Subheading">Where and how</h3>
        <p><strong>Remote only.</strong> We have no office. The company is incorporated in Estonia.</p>
        <p><strong>Full-time contractors.</strong> You invoice us through your own company or through Deel.</p>
        <p><strong>Anywhere with overlap.</strong> Work from anywhere with at least 4 hours' overlap with Central European Time. Some roles also need overlap with the US East Coast.</p>
        <p><strong>English</strong> is our working language.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3 class="mf-Subheading">What we expect</h3>
        <p><strong>Flexibility with accountability.</strong> You decide how and when to do the work, and you own the result.</p>
        <p><strong>One cloud, one stack.</strong> We work on AWS with Terraform and ECS. Doing the same kind of work many times is how we get better at it each time.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mf-Section">
  <div class="mf-Inner">
    <h2 class="mf-Heading mf-Heading_Center">Our story</h2>
    <figure class="mf-Photo">
      <img src="/meet-fivexl/santa-queue-2019.jpg" alt="The queue for Santa's house in Las Palmas, 21 December 2019, with a stroller in the foreground" width="840" height="1120" loading="lazy">
      <figcaption>Las Palmas, 21 December 2019. The queue where FivexL started.</figcaption>
    </figure>
    <ol class="mf-Timeline" role="list">
      <li>
        <span class="mf-Year">2019</span>
        <div>
          <h3>The queue for Santa</h3>
          <p>On 21 December, standing in the queue for Santa's house in Las Palmas with Andrey's daughter, Andrey and Artem decide to join forces. Anastasia joins them as our third co-founder, to build the brand and the marketing. The first project runs on AWS and HashiStack.</p>
          <p class="mf-Count">Consultants: 2</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="mf-Year">2020</span>
        <div>
          <h3>The rules</h3>
          <p>The rules: stay small, stay cosy, stick out, stay niche, know your customer and say no to the rest, keep learning, give people a chance.</p>
          <p>The customer and stack: remote only, startups, AWS, Terraform, security as job zero.</p>
          <p>The first summer practice brings in Vladimir (our fourth co-founder) and Ivan. Olga joins as project assistant and later becomes our financial and administrative manager. We open-source cloudtrail-to-slack.</p>
          <p class="mf-Count">Consultants: 5 · Revenue: €168k</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="mf-Year">2021</span>
        <div>
          <h3>AWS only</h3>
          <p>We drop HashiStack and go all in on AWS and ECS, reusing what works. Alexey joins through the second summer practice. Andrey joins AWS Community Builders.</p>
          <p class="mf-Count">Consultants: 6 · Revenue: €362k</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="mf-Year">2022</span>
        <div>
          <h3>Growing up</h3>
          <p>Vera joins to run marketing. The blog launches and the website gets rebuilt. First in-person get-together, in Turkey. Vladimir joins AWS Community Builders. First product prototypes. We open-source lprobe.</p>
          <p class="mf-Count">Consultants: 6 · Revenue: €498k</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="mf-Year">2023</span>
        <div>
          <h3>Stabilization</h3>
          <p>First fixed-price, fixed-scope project. Anton joins. SSO Elevator goes open source. Work starts on Right Start.</p>
          <p class="mf-Count">Consultants: 5 · Revenue: €523k</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="mf-Year">2024</span>
        <div>
          <h3>Acceleration</h3>
          <p>Guilherme joins. Right Start ships to its first customer as a productized landing zone. AWS Advanced Tier. AWS Summit talks. At our get-together in Las Palmas, Vladimir becomes a partner and takes on business development.</p>
          <p class="mf-Count">Consultants: 6 · Revenue: €737k</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="mf-Year">2025</span>
        <div>
          <h3>From services to products</h3>
          <p>Anastasia takes on the Marketing Director role. Berit and Aleksandr join. Customers buy Right Start and the ECS blueprint as products. Anton moves into a full-time consulting role. Work starts on our own product. We narrow our focus to US health tech companies that handle patient data.</p>
          <p class="mf-Count">Consultants: 8 · Revenue: €987k</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="mf-Year">2026</span>
        <div>
          <h3>Growing past ten</h3>
          <p>We become a services partner in Anthropic's Claude Partner Network and start AI forward-deployed engineering. Fernando and Gilbert join as consultants, and Bhavika joins through our internship. We grow beyond ten, still hiring only experts, which is why it takes time.</p>
          <p class="mf-Count">Consultants: 11 (14 people in total)</p>
        </div>
      </li>
    </ol>
  </div>
</section>
